# Page Blueprints

Section-by-section starting shapes. Adapt them: drop any section with nothing real to say,
and reorder when the audience needs a different sequence. A shorter honest page beats a
complete-looking padded one.

Every blueprint below assumes the copy rules in SKILL.md — benefit-led headlines, grade-eight
language, action-verb CTAs, `[YOUR INPUT]` instead of invented proof.

## Home

The home page has one job: get the right visitor to the one conversion action, fast.

1. **Hero** — benefit-led headline (under 12 words), one supporting sentence naming who it is
   for and what changes for them, the primary CTA. Nothing competes with that CTA here.
2. **Who this is for** — two or three lines, or a short list, so the wrong visitor
   self-selects out and the right one feels recognised.
3. **The problem** — name the pain in the customer's own words. This is where the user's
   interview answers about objections earn their keep.
4. **What you get** — the offer as outcomes, three to five items. Each item leads with the
   result, then names the feature that delivers it.
5. **How it works** — three or four steps. Reduces the fear of an unclear process, which is a
   top reason people leave without acting.
6. **Proof** — real testimonials, named clients, real numbers, credentials. If none were
   supplied, keep the section with `[YOUR INPUT]` slots and tell the owner plainly that this
   section carries more conversion weight than anything else on the page.
7. **Objection handling / short FAQ** — three to five questions, pulled from what prospects
   actually ask.
8. **Closing CTA** — repeat the same action, same verb, no new offer.

Secondary links (blog, careers, socials) live in the footer, not above the fold.

## About

About is not autobiography — visitors read it to decide whether to trust the person behind the
offer. Write it as credibility, not chronology.

1. **Headline** — the reason to trust them, not the word "About".
2. **Short story** — why they do this work, in their voice. Two or three short paragraphs.
3. **Credentials and experience** — only what is real and verifiable.
4. **Values or working principles** — three to four, each one sentence, tied to what the client
   experiences rather than abstract virtue.
5. **Who they work with** — reinforces fit.
6. **Photo slot** — a real face outperforms a stock image; note it if they lack one.
7. **CTA** — the same primary action as the home page.

## Services

Choose one shape and say why:
- **One page, all services** — for three or fewer offers, or when buyers compare them side by
  side.
- **One page per service** — for distinct audiences or distinct search intent per offer. Better
  for SEO, more work to maintain.

Per service: benefit-led title, who it is for, the outcome, what is included, how it runs,
price or price range (or "starting at" / `[YOUR INPUT]`), and a CTA. Keep the order of services
matched to what the owner most wants to sell.

## Pricing

Only build this page if the user gave real prices or is willing to show ranges — a pricing page
with no prices erodes trust.

Per tier: name, who it suits, price, what is included, one CTA. Add a short note on what
changes the price when the work is custom. Address the two questions buyers always have:
what happens if it is not a fit, and what the payment terms are.

## Contact / Book

Keep it to one action. Ask only for the fields the owner actually needs — every extra field
costs completions.

Include: the action-verb headline, the form or booking embed slot, expected response time,
alternative contact method, location or service area, and what to expect after submitting.

## Case Studies / Portfolio

Never invent a result. With permission and real data, use: client and context → the problem →
what was done → the outcome (real numbers only) → a client quote if supplied. Without real
data, deliver the structure with `[YOUR INPUT]` markers and note that unverified case studies
are a legal and reputational risk, not just a copy gap.

## Blog / Insights

Provide the index structure (title, date, excerpt, category, read time) and a starter list of
five to eight article topics drawn from the audience's real questions. Each topic gets a
working title and the search intent it serves. Blog pages feed both SEO and AEO — see
`seo-aeo-pack.md`.

## FAQ

Ten to fifteen questions, written the way prospects phrase them out loud, each answered in two
to four sentences with the answer in the first sentence. This page is the highest-leverage AEO
surface on the site; the structured-data markup for it lives in `seo-aeo-pack.md`.

Cover: price and payment, timeline, process, what is not included, guarantees or refund policy,
who it is not for, and how to start.

## Legal

Note which are needed — privacy policy, terms of service, cookie notice, refund policy — based
on whether the site collects data, sells, or serves regions with specific requirements. Provide
the section outline and flag that final wording should be reviewed by someone qualified in the
owner's jurisdiction. Do not draft binding legal language as if it were vetted.
