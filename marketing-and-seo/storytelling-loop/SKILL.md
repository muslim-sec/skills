# Storytelling Loop — Dopamine Addiction Engine

## Overview
This skill restructures any raw content (transcript, news brief, topic description, technical report) into a psychologically compelling narrative using the **Dopamine Addiction Loop** — the same 4-step framework casinos use to keep people gambling and top creators use to make content impossible to skip.

> [!IMPORTANT]  
> This is a **universal content skill**. It is NOT limited to any specific platform,  
> niche, or medium. It works for tweets, YouTube scripts, podcast intros, blog  
> posts, LinkedIn articles, newsletters, and any written or spoken content.

---

## The 4-Step Core Framework

Every piece of content you generate using this skill MUST pass through these 4 steps **in order**. Before writing the final output, you MUST first create a hidden structural outline mapping the raw input to each step.

### Step 1: The Stakes 🎰
> *"To have any dopamine release, the viewer has to care about the story in the first place."*

Before anything else, establish **why the audience should care**.  
Stakes require exactly three ingredients:

| Ingredient | Definition | Example (Cyber) | Example (General) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A Character** | Someone the audience can root for (often the reader themselves) | "Your company's CISO" / "You, the developer" | "A small business owner" / "Your 5-year-old daughter" |
| **Something at Risk** | A tangible loss or gain | "Your entire customer database" / "Your career" | "Your life savings" / "The family home" |
| **Urgency** | A time pressure or ticking clock | "This exploit is active RIGHT NOW" | "You have 48 hours before..." |

**Rule:** If the raw input has no obvious character, **make the reader the character**. If there is no urgency, **manufacture it** from the implications (e.g., "Every hour you wait, 10,000 more devices get infected").

---

### Step 2: The Big Question ❓
> *"The stakes make them pay attention, but the big question makes them super curious and locked in."*

After establishing stakes, plant a **specific, irresistible question** in the audience's mind. This is the curiosity gap — the thing they MUST know the answer to.

**Rules:**
- The Big Question must be **implied, not stated directly** in most cases. Let the context force it into the reader's brain.
- It should feel like the "next card in Blackjack" — the audience is leaning forward.
- For short-form: The Big Question IS the hook line.
- For long-form: The Big Question appears in the first 10% of the content.

**Examples:**
| Raw Input | The Big Question (Implied) |
|---|---|
| "New LLM beats GPT-5 on reasoning" | "Wait... what model? And how is that even possible?" |
| "Critical Windows RCE, CVSS 9.8" | "Am I affected? Can someone already exploit this?" |
| "AI agent books $200k/month in sales" | "How? And can I build one too?" |

---

### Step 3: The Headfake 🃏
> *"The headfake is where you contrast against what the normal viewer should believe."*

This is the **twist** — the moment you subvert the audience's expectation. You set up what they THINK the answer is, then reveal a surprising contrast.

**Rules:**
- The Headfake must feel like a genuine "Wait, WHAT?" moment.
- It works by **contrast**: "You'd think X... but actually Y."
- It must be factually accurate — never fabricate a twist. Find the real counter-intuitive angle in the source material.
- If no natural headfake exists in the data, find the **least obvious implication** and lead with that.

**Pattern Templates:**
- "Everyone assumes [common belief]... but the data shows [surprising truth]."
- "The obvious fix is [X]. But that's exactly what makes it worse."
- "You'd think [big company] would catch this. They didn't. Here's why."
- "This isn't a bug. It's a feature that was designed this way on purpose."

---

### Step 4: The Rehook 🔄
> *"Just like a casino tries to redeal the next Blackjack hand as soon as possible, you want your stories to restart this loop again as quickly as you can."*

After delivering the Headfake payoff, **immediately open the next loop** or drive the audience to the next action.

**Rules by Medium:**

| Medium | Rehook Strategy |
|---|---|
| **Single Tweet** | End with a CTA that opens a new curiosity gap (e.g., "Want to see the 3 other flaws they missed? Bookmark this 🔖") |
| **Twitter Thread** | Each tweet in the thread ends with a mini-rehook leading to the next tweet. The final tweet has the CTA. |
| **YouTube Script** | Resolve the Headfake, then immediately say "But that's not even the scary part..." to open Loop #2. |
| **Newsletter / Blog** | Use a section transition that implies the next section has even bigger stakes. |
| **Podcast Script** | Use a vocal pivot: "Now, that was wild. But wait until you hear what happened NEXT..." |

## Medium-Specific Compression Rules

### Short-Form (Tweets, Shorts, Reels Captions)
Compress the entire loop into 3 beats:
1. **Line 1 (Stakes + Big Question):** One punchy sentence that establishes risk AND plants curiosity.  
2. **Line 2–3 (Headfake):** The twist or counter-intuitive reveal.  
3. **Final Line (Rehook):** The CTA or next curiosity gap.

### Long-Form (YouTube, Podcasts, Newsletters, Blogs)
Cascade **multiple loops**. Structure:
- **Loop 1:** Stakes → Big Question → Headfake → Rehook (opens Loop 2)
- **Loop 2:** New Stakes → New Big Question → New Headfake → Rehook (opens Loop 3 or final CTA)
- Great long-form content has **3–5 cascading loops**.

## Critical Constraints

### 1. Show, Don't Tell (MANDATORY)
> [!CAUTION]  
> You are **STRICTLY FORBIDDEN** from using the framework's terminology in your  
> output. NEVER write "The stakes are...", "The big question is...",  
> "Here's the headfake...", or "Time to rehook." These words are for YOUR  
> internal planning only. The audience must FEEL the psychology, never SEE it.

### 2. Structural Integrity Check
Before writing the final output, you MUST internally verify:
- [ ] Does the opening establish stakes within the first 2 sentences?
- [ ] Is there a clear curiosity gap that makes the reader NEED to keep going?
- [ ] Is there at least one genuine "Wait, what?" moment (Headfake)?
- [ ] Does the ending either open a new loop or drive a specific action (Rehook)?
- [ ] Are the framework step names completely invisible in the final text?

### 3. Authenticity Over Manipulation
The framework amplifies genuinely interesting information. It does NOT fabricate drama. If the source material is boring, the Headfake should be "here's the angle you missed" — not invented shock value.

## Common Mistakes
1. **Being too explicit:** Writing "Here's the twist..." instead of letting the contrast speak for itself.
2. **Skipping Stakes:** Jumping straight to information without making the reader care first.
3. **Weak Rehooks:** Ending content with "That's it!" instead of opening a new curiosity gap or driving an action.
